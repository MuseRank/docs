Publishing content is only half the job; keeping it healthy is the other half. The Content Audit analyzes your existing articles and surfaces two kinds of problems worth fixing: content that's gone stale, and pages that are competing with each other.

## What the audit finds

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Refresh candidates" icon="rotate">
    Articles that are aging or slipping in performance and would benefit from an update — new information, better optimization, or expanded sections.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Cannibalization" icon="people-arrows">
    Cases where two or more of your pages target the same keyword and end up competing with each other, splitting their ranking potential.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Why refreshing matters

Search rankings decay. An article that ranked well a year ago can fall as competitors publish fresher content and as the topic evolves. Refreshing a proven article is often a higher-return move than writing a brand-new one — it already has authority and history, so updates can quickly recover or improve its position.

## Why cannibalization hurts

When multiple pages chase the same keyword, search engines aren't sure which to rank — so neither performs as well as a single, focused page would. Resolving cannibalization (by consolidating, differentiating, or re-targeting one of the pages) concentrates your ranking power where it counts.

## Using the audit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Content Audit">
    Select it from the sidebar to see the audit for your current website.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review refresh candidates">
    Work through the flagged articles. Open one, update it in the [editor](/guides/editor), and re-publish.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resolve cannibalization">
    For competing pages, decide which should own the keyword. Consolidate the others, re-point them at a different target, or differentiate their angles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Re-check performance">
    After changes, monitor results in [Search Performance](/guides/search-performance) to confirm the fix worked.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Make the audit a recurring habit. A monthly pass to refresh a few aging articles compounds over time and protects the rankings you've already earned.
</Tip>

<Note>
  The audit is most powerful when [Google Search Console is connected](/integrations/google-search-console), since real performance data sharpens which articles truly need attention.
</Note>
