Your **content plan** is MuseRank's strategic view of what to write. Rather than picking topics ad hoc, MuseRank analyzes your website and produces a prioritized set of winnable topics, organized into a [topical map](/guides/topical-maps). The plan is created during [onboarding](/onboarding) and evolves as your site and search landscape change.

<Note>
  The content plan isn't a separate sidebar item — it powers your [Topical Map](/guides/topical-maps) and content queue, and you act on it from the [dashboard](/dashboard) when updates are ready to review.
</Note>

## How the plan is built

MuseRank generates the plan through an automated pipeline:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Extract seed topics" icon="seedling">
    MuseRank gathers seed keywords from your [brand voice profile](/onboarding), your site's pages and headings, navigation, and (for stores) your products.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find winnable keywords" icon="magnifying-glass">
    It expands those seeds into keyword ideas and keeps only the **winnable** ones — low competition, meaningful search volume, and a stable or growing trend. Impossible or fading terms are filtered out.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cluster into a structure" icon="diagram-project">
    The surviving keywords are grouped by search intent and clustered into a hierarchy of **pillars**, **clusters**, and **subclusters** — the backbone of [topical authority](/guides/topical-maps).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prioritize the topics" icon="list-ol">
    Each topic becomes a plan item with a **priority score** (based on volume, difficulty, and intent), so the highest-value topics rise to the top of your queue.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The result is a draft topical map plus an ordered list of topics ready to turn into articles.

## When the plan updates

The plan isn't static. MuseRank can refresh it:

- **During onboarding** — your initial plan is generated automatically.
- **On demand** — re-scan to regenerate the plan when your strategy shifts.
- **From new signals** — for example, when fresh search data suggests new opportunities or overlaps.

When a refresh produces changes, they're staged for your review rather than applied silently.

## Reviewing plan updates

When there are pending changes, the [dashboard](/dashboard) surfaces a prompt to **review content plan updates**. The review screen groups changes by type so you can decide what to keep:

| Change type | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **New opportunities** | Winnable topics MuseRank discovered to add to your plan |
| **New overlaps** | Topics that may overlap with existing content (potential [cannibalization](/guides/content-audit)) |
| **Score changes** | Topics whose priority shifted as data changed |
| **Removed topics** | Topics no longer recommended |
| **Reclassified** | Topics moved within the pillar/cluster hierarchy |

For each change you can:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Accept" icon="check">
    Apply the change to your plan.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ignore" icon="xmark">
    Dismiss it and keep your plan as-is.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Defer" icon="clock">
    Skip it for now and revisit later.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

You can resolve items individually or use **Accept all changes** to apply everything at once.

### Versions and your edits

Accepting changes creates a **new version** of your plan, so its history is preserved. Manual adjustments you've made — removing a topic, reordering the queue, or merging clusters — are remembered and carried forward into future versions, so a refresh never quietly undoes your decisions.
